## Step 0 — What the interviewer wants

You keep ISRs short and know how to defer without losing events.

## Step 1 — Core answer (bullets)

- ISR: no malloc, printf, blocking, long loops
- Defer via flag, ring buffer, or workqueue
- Threaded IRQ: top half acks hardware; bottom half can sleep (I2C/SPI)
- GPIO encoder: hard IRQ for timestamp; threaded for debounce/filter

## Step 2 — Traps / mistakes

- Heavy parsing in ISR
- Mutex in ISR
- Missing irq_save around shared list edits

## Step 3 — Follow-ups with answers

**Q: GPIO encoder edges?**  
**A:** Hard IRQ captures timestamp; threaded handler debounces and computes delta.

**Q: Lost interrupts?**  
**A:** Check NVIC priority, clear flags early, ring overflow policy.

## Step 4 — 60-second script

ISRs do the minimum: clear hardware, enqueue data, wake a task. Anything that blocks or takes milliseconds belongs in a bottom half — workqueue or threaded IRQ. For a fast encoder I capture edges in hard IRQ for jitter, then process position in a thread where I can use I2C if needed.

## Step 5 — TI story hook

EnDAT position ISR only latched timer and pushed to ring; frame parse ran on R5F — kept ISR under 2 µs.
